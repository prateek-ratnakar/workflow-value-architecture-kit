# Business capability map

A capability is what the business must be able to do, stated independently of who does it or which system supports it. The map is the stable anchor: org charts and systems change, capabilities rarely do. Heat-map it three ways before any AI discussion - strategic importance, current performance, and AI opportunity.

## Level 1 and Level 2 (consumer goods and retail enterprise, illustrative)

| L1 capability | L2 capabilities |
|---|---|
| Customer and market management | Market sensing, pricing and promotion management, customer account management, trade terms |
| Order to cash | Order capture, credit management, fulfilment coordination, billing, collections, cash application, deduction and dispute management |
| Supply and fulfilment | Demand planning, inventory management, logistics and carrier management, returns |
| Source to pay | Sourcing and contracting, supplier management, purchasing, receiving, invoice processing, payment |
| Record to report | General ledger, close and consolidation, reconciliation, management and statutory reporting, tax |
| Hire to retire | Workforce planning, talent acquisition, onboarding, payroll, performance, learning, offboarding |
| Risk, compliance and trust | Policy management, fraud and abuse controls, regulatory compliance, audit |
| Enterprise enablement | Data and analytics, technology platforms, program and portfolio management |

## How to use it

1. Score each L2 on strategic importance (1-5) and current performance (1-5). High importance with low performance is where transformation starts.
2. Overlay AI opportunity from the process-family step scores in `ai-readiness/`.
3. Only then map capabilities to org units and systems - that is where ownership gaps and duplicate effort show up.

A capability that three organizations each partly own, with no single owner, is usually the first redesign candidate.
